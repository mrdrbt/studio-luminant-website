Hocket

We wove waving horizontal bands through straight upright ones. Each waving band rides over several upright bands, then dips under for a short stretch, and the surface reads like a closely woven cloth.

The pattern

A hocket is a medieval way of splitting a melody between two voices, one falling silent as the other comes in. The bands of Hocket take turns in the same way: a waving band runs over five to seven upright bands, dips under one to three, and comes back to the surface.

Fine grooves split every band into strips; the waving bands are 100 mm wide, the upright ones 75 mm. The waving bands narrow and widen along their run, so no band keeps the same width for long. The relief reaches 16 mm.

How it reads in light

The image at the top of the page is a render computed from this design’s depth map; on a computer the light follows your cursor, on a phone it moves as you scroll. With the light at the side, the edge of each band on top throws a shadow onto the band beneath and the weave gains depth, while the fine grooves pick up the light as close-set lines. As the light turns head-on the shadows shorten and the weave softens into texture.

On the wall

Hocket is designed as a 600 × 600 mm panel. The weave is not cut at the panel edges: laid side by side and stacked, the bands pass from one panel into the next and the wall reads as a single surface. Where the pattern is cut at the edges of the wall is set out on the layout drawing.
