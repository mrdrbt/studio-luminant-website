What Is an Architectural
Relief Panel?

A relief panel is an architectural element that turns a wall from a flat surface into a three-dimensional one. What it is, where it's used, what it's made from, and how it's ordered.

Studio Luminant · Intro Guide · 6 min read

The character of a space is often set by its walls. An architectural relief panel is the way to turn that wall from a flat covering into a three-dimensional surface that reads by light. This guide outlines what a relief panel is and how an architect brings one into a project.

What Is a Relief Panel?

An architectural relief panel is a cast wall panel that carries a raised (relief) pattern or texture on its surface. The pattern is not printed onto or glued over the panel; it is cast into the body of the panel itself. Because of this, the relief reads through shadow and highlight as the angle of light shifts — a depth effect a flat print can never produce.

Relief panels can be a standard, series-produced product like a tile, or — as Studio Luminant focuses on — bespoke, large-format panels made to the project. In the latter, the motif, format, and surface are defined directly around the project.

Where Is It Used?

Relief panels are used most in commercial and hospitality spaces where a surface needs to be memorable: hotel lobbies and reception backdrops, restaurant and bar walls, retail feature surfaces, lift lobbies, and meeting or event spaces. In residential projects they are chosen as a single feature wall in areas such as the living room or entrance. The common thread is that the panel becomes the focus of the design as a feature surface.

What Material Is It Made From?

Studio Luminant casts its relief panels in two mineral materials. Lumina PMAG™ for interior applications; Lumina PUCOMP™ for exterior, wet-area, or high-detail work. Both materials accept the same artisan finishes, so the surface appearance can be set independently of the material. The material decision is made on weight and application context.

How It Differs from a Modular Tile

A relief panel and a modular decorative tile are often confused, yet their architectural results differ. The comparison below summarises how a bespoke relief panel separates from a tiled surface.

How Do You Order One?

A bespoke relief panel is a decision made at the design stage, and generally follows these steps: brief and context, motif development, material selection, a physical sample, format and m², surface finish, BIM documents, and fixing. You can find the full process, set out in eight detailed steps, in our How to Specify Custom Cast Wall Panels guide.

Practical Note

The effect of a relief surface does not fully read on a render. Before committing to a project, requesting a physical sample is the only reliable way to see how the surface catches the light.
