Mezzavoce

We drew an irregular net of cells in many sizes. Each cell rises from its joint line into a broad, rounded crown, so the surface reads as cushions set side by side.

The pattern

Mezza voce means singing at half voice, without pushing the sound. The surface keeps the same restraint: there is no hard edge anywhere, and every cell swells gently from its rim to its middle.

The cells are not one size. Large ones are scattered among small ones, and the net never settles into a straight row in any direction. The relief reaches 18 mm: the crowns are the highest points of the surface, the joint lines the deepest.

How it reads in light

The image at the top of the page is in live light. With the light at the side, one flank of each cell catches it, the other falls into shadow, and the joint lines show as a fine, broken net. As the light turns head-on the shadows shorten and the surface settles into a pale, soft texture.

On the wall

Mezzavoce is designed as a 1200 × 600 mm panel. The net is not cut at the panel edges: laid side by side and stacked, the cells pass from one panel into the next and the wall reads as a single surface. Where the net is cut at the edges of the wall is set out on the layout drawing.
