Mezzavoce

We drew an irregular net of cells in many sizes. Each cell rises from its joint line into a broad, rounded crown, so the surface reads as cushions set side by side.

The pattern

Mezza voce means singing at half voice, without pushing the sound. The surface keeps the same restraint: there is no hard edge anywhere, and every cell swells gently from its rim to its middle.

The cells are not one size. Large ones are scattered among small ones, and the net never settles into a straight row in any direction. The relief reaches 18 mm: the crowns are the highest points of the surface, the joint lines the deepest.

How it reads in light

The image at the top of the page is a render computed from this design’s depth map; on a computer the light follows your cursor, on a phone it moves as you scroll. With the light at the side, one flank of each cell catches it, the other falls into shadow, and the joint lines show as a fine, broken net. As the light turns head-on the shadows shorten and the surface settles into a pale, soft texture.

On the wall

Mezzavoce is designed as a 1200 × 600 mm panel. The net is not cut at the panel edges: laid side by side and stacked, the cells pass from one panel into the next and the wall reads as a single surface. Where the net is cut at the edges of the wall is set out on the layout drawing.

Where it works

Mezzavoce’s network forms no straight row in any direction, so the wall reads as a soft texture rather than a pattern and can cover a whole wall. In raking light the seams show as a fine, broken net; in head-on light the surface settles into a calm, even texture. Pieces come paint-ready or with an artisan finish; a paint-ready surface can be painted on site to match the room.

Frequently asked questions

What size are Mezzavoce panels, and how are they laid?

Mezzavoce is designed as a 1200 × 600 mm relief wall panel. The cells are not all one size: the large ones are scattered among the small, and the network runs continuous across the panel joints. Laid side by side and one above another, the panels read as one surface. The layout drawing sets where the pattern is cut at the edges of the wall.

How deep is the Mezzavoce relief?

From the crown of each cell down to the seams, the relief is 18 mm deep; each cell swells gently from its edge to its centre.

What is the lead time for Mezzavoce, and how is it fixed?

Lead time is 4 weeks after contract and advance for orders up to 50 m²; for larger orders the quote sets the date. Delivery is ex-works Sapanca (EXW). Pieces are hand-cast in Lumina PMAG™ — Polymer Modified Composite Gypsum and fixed to the wall with a polymer adhesive, or with a rail system on metal stud and plasterboard walls. Pieces that wrap a column, turn a corner or follow a curved wall are cast as Bespoke, in the same motif. The minimum order is 7.5 m². The mould is made for your order and you approve the master before casting; both are included in the 4 weeks.
