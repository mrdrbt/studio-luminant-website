Case study · Private residence, Istanbul

A wall from one photograph

The interior designer brought a single photograph of a coursed relief wall. We traced its profile, rebuilt it as two moulds and cast the wall for a living room in Istanbul.

The brief

Tufetto were finishing a living room and had one image in mind: a wall built up in courses, each block cut with soft concave grooves that hold a shadow when the light comes from the side. They had a photograph, not a drawing, and no supplier who made it.

From photograph to profile

We looked for a repeat in the photograph and found none: the wall reads as hand-laid ashlar, every block slightly different. So we traced the crest lines of two courses and rebuilt the profile by rule. Every face is either flat or a concave cove, never convex, and no cove runs deeper than 40 mm.

Two 600 × 400 mm masters carry the whole wall. Each is laid straight or turned 180°, which gives four different blocks, and a seeded running bond places them so no repeat shows across the 2.6 m height.

The designer's revision

We sent the exact model into Tufetto's own 3D scene so the wall could be judged in their room, at their camera. The note came back on one face: the widest flat block should be wider still. We widened it by 40%, gave the width back from the neighbouring coves so the plate stayed at 600 mm, and only then cut the masters.

Making

Both masters were CNC-machined, and the production moulds taken from them in silicone. The plates were cast and finished by our team in Sakarya, then laid to a numbered plan, course by course, with every cut piece kept above 90 mm.

One wall, one day of light

One camera on a tripod, six frames from morning to evening. Nothing moved but the sun.
