Calculated light, not photographs

Each square shows the same 600 × 600 mm of surface, at one scale. Your browser works the image out live from the design’s own height data, so the shadows redraw as the light moves. The figure beside a name is the relief depth.

Try the set-back spot, which strikes the wall at 55°: Mezzavoce drops to a third of its contrast while Andante holds. Fugato wants its light from the side: a ceiling wash leaves it quiet, a side window brings it up.

The calculation assumes one matte tone. An artisan finish and the other light sources in a room will change what you see.

The designs

For a café, restaurant or hotel wall, see the designs in rooms in the Gallery.

  • AccelerandoWe drew a field of cells whose size grades across the wall.
  • AndanteWe drew a field of horizontal ribs and pulled it up or down along four vertical lines, the way a comb is drawn through marbling.
  • AntiphonWe drew vertical bands whose two edges wave against each other, so each band narrows and widens into a chain of almond shapes; where one band narrows, its neighbour widens.
  • FiorituraA floral field we drew in the vocabulary of Ottoman tile ornament: tulip buds, rosettes and serrated leaves, linked by winding stems.
  • FugatoWe drew a profile of coves and flat bands and divided it across two moulds.
  • GlissandoA surface of drifting fibres.
  • GruppettoOne fan-shaped form, designed by us and laid in long S-curves.
  • HemiolaA wall of blocks, each with an opening turned off square, drawn to read as hand-set.
  • HocketWe wove waving horizontal bands through straight upright ones.
  • MelismaA maze of fine lines that we let run the length of the wall as one current.
  • MezzavoceWe drew an irregular net of cells in many sizes.
  • RicercarA lattice built from the Gothic quatrefoil.
  • RondeauOne round form, drawn once and laid as a field.
  • SonadoFive pleats to a panel.
